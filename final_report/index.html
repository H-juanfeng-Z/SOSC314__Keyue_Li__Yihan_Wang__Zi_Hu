Help-seeking, response speed and answer helpfulness on Stack Overflow¶

Zi Hu · Keyue Li · Yihan Wang — SOSC314

Project repository

Updated 18 October 2026.

1. Research question and motivation¶

Do different requests receive different help? A quick response can miss the request, while a delayed answer can resolve it. We examine help-seeking intention, technical topic, first-answer helpfulness and speed in historical Python questions.

We test sensitivity to question composition and scoring prompts.

2. Data and provenance¶

The Python Questions from Stack Overflow archive contains Questions, Answers and Tags from 2008–2016. IDs connect each question to its first answer, ordered by creation time then answer ID. This measures initial help rather than the best eventual answer.

Three disjoint batches contain 5,000 questions: 4,986 usable coarse ratings, eight context skips and six invalid records. Fine scoring covers 1,000 questions nested in the newest 2,000; 993 have all three scores. Missing outcomes remain missing.

Technical-topic comparisons below use the newest batch, which has complete topic coverage: 1,992 valid coarse ratings and 993 three-scale pairs. Partner LDA exports cover 4,977 valid coarse questions and 991 fine pairs, including 1,557 and 307 recovered labels. The author reports using the same archived LDA model, 694-phrase vocabulary and topic mapping for both tiers. Strict labels use the Kaggle snapshot; recovered labels use current Stack Exchange API text, approximately 62% of which was edited after 2016. This introduces a temporal mismatch. Counts before filtering differ from valid-score denominators. Pooled coarse summaries consistently use the unified baseline scoring version.

We avoid contributor profiling and reproduce short diagnostic content. Edits, deleted posts and public-participation selection limit inference. Answered-only sampling cannot establish the quality of help that unanswered requests might receive.

3. Problem setup and assumptions¶

The supervised benchmark predicts an answer within 24 hours from question characteristics and text. The observational analysis instead relates question groups to annotated helpfulness of existing answers.

We distinguish technical subject matter from expressed intention. Seven intention decisions may overlap; partner LDA assigns a dominant request category. These classifications are not interchangeable. We assume the archived text permits a meaningful assessment of visible assistance, while acknowledging uncertain historical context. Means of 0–10 scores are descriptive summaries that treat ordinal steps as equally spaced; they are not calibrated units of utility.

4. Methods¶

For response prediction, structured logistic regression provides a baseline. TF–IDF plus logistic regression adds lexical information, while frozen MiniLM representations plus logistic regression add a dense text representation. Encoders were not fine-tuned in this benchmark. A temporal split trains through 2014, validates on 2015 and tests on 2016, reducing dependence on randomly mixing adjacent historical observations.

Technical grouping uses TF–IDF plus NMF on 20,000 questions. NMF produces inspectable topic-word weights. At K=8, retaining code gives greater normalized dominant-topic weight (0.601 versus 0.542) and fewer ambiguous assignments (35.5% versus 46.3%, weight below 0.5). HTML/URL handling also differs, so this is not an isolated code-removal effect. Eight broad topics are refined by subdividing four, yielding thirteen final categories. Names and subdivisions follow inspection, without a formal independent labeling evaluation.

Doc2Vec plus k-means is a dense-document baseline. PV-DBOW uses 100 dimensions and 20 epochs; an alternative PV-DM configuration uses 200 dimensions and 50 epochs. Across K=4–10, saved baseline silhouette scores range 0.006–0.023 and the alternative −0.041–−0.004. Weak separation under that metric, alongside NMF's interpretability, supports retaining NMF for exploration; it does not demonstrate universal superiority. LDA separately models up to three verb phrases per question with ten archived topics mapped to five request categories.

Qwen2.5-Coder-14B-Instruct annotates posts; it does not generate the Stack Overflow answers evaluated. The inspected expansion runner uses local float16 inference on remote NVIDIA L20 GPUs, greedy decoding and seed 314, without fine-tuning. Inputs above 7,500 tokens are skipped, not truncated. Output limits are 256 new tokens for coarse/intent judgments and 512 for fine judgments. We chose this coding-oriented model for programming-text annotation and feasible repeated local inference; we did not establish that it is the most accurate annotator. Saved environments differ across servers; the exact upstream weight revision is not pinned.

Question-only prompts assess each intention as yes, no or uncertain, requiring a supplied question-line citation for positive decisions. Helpfulness prompts receive both question and numbered answer lines. The coarse protocol returns relevance, actionability and sufficiency on 0–2 scales, plus a separate overall judgment: unhelpful, partial, substantial or uncertain. Overall is not a mechanical sum.

The candidate 0–10 protocol also states the request, provided help and remaining gap. Anchors distinguish no usable help, partial assistance, adequate coverage and complete directly usable assistance. Format and Scope share anchors; Scope adds instructions to respect the expressed request instead of demanding an entire implementation. This comparison is therefore prompt sensitivity, not a pure test of the number of scale points. JSON and citation checks establish protocol validity, not correctness. The runner neither executes answer code nor retrieves linked contents.

The workflow distinguishes intention from helpfulness. The method diagnostics support interpretability and metric separation, not validated category accuracy. Full scoring and intention prompts retain the exact inspected instructions.

Text features improve discrimination relative to the structured baseline, but better prediction does not explain why a response arrives or establish its quality. Although answer-derived predictors were excluded, later question edits may leak information unavailable at posting time.

For helpfulness, topic means vary on identical paired support. These are descriptive contrasts with unequal group sizes, not a league table. Exploratory adjustment includes year indicators, log question length and code presence. The Python-basics versus API/server substantial-rating gap falls from 8.21 percentage points to 4.40; its adjusted pointwise 95% interval is −1.10 to 9.90 points. No adjusted contrast excludes zero under the conservative 96-contrast multiplicity rule. This does not prove equivalence: adjustment is incomplete and simultaneous intervals sacrifice precision.

First-answer delays are skewed, so we emphasize medians. API-change questions have a 60.26-minute median versus 24.41 for API usage. The 35.85-minute difference has a whole-question bootstrap pointwise 95% interval of −4.16 to 60.03 minutes. Resampling questions preserves overlapping intentions. The observed gap is suggestive, not a reliable claim that all API questions receive slower responses; API change is a specific subgroup.

On identical pairs, higher coarse categories correspond to higher fine means, but each category still spans fine scores. These are two different measurement protocols, not a conversion formula.

Prompt sensitivity is more clearly visible: on 993 identical pairs, Scope exceeds Format by 0.407 points on average, with a paired bootstrap 95% interval of 0.262–0.545. There are 277 increases, 126 decreases and 590 ties. In pooled LDA labels, do-not-work leads Format and way-of-using leads Scope. On the 684 strict-only fine pairs, way-of-using leads both. For example, do-not-work's Format mean falls from 6.72 (pooled n=61) to 6.51 (strict n=43), while way-of-using changes from 6.63 (n=331) to 6.61 (n=220). Ranking is sensitive to label provenance as well as prompting; restriction changes the sample and does not identify a causal recovery effect. Top coarse scores are concentrated, but this alone cannot attribute rank changes to scale resolution. The fine protocol also changes wording and provides eleven integer values, 0 through 10.

6. Error analysis and diagnostics¶

In question 19851005, a local change resolves the index-name request. Format and Scope assign 10 and 9 despite identical stored request/help/gap/reason text; the output gives no substantive basis for the one-point change.

Question 21352016 requests two coordinates per row and an array output. The answer parses a different input representation and returns lists/tuples. A GPT-assisted audit flags both issues, which the model explanations overlook despite scores increasing from 6 to 7. Historical runtime and edits remain uncertain. These cases come from a separate GPT-assisted review of 48 cases stratified by score change and cannot estimate population error rates. The authors also manually inspected many questions, answers and ratings during development. Their checks were informal, without a uniform adjudication standard or recorded accuracy benchmark; they should not be conflated with the 48 GPT-assisted cases.

7. Interpretation and substantive takeaways¶

Narrow library requests may permit reusable solutions, whereas API problems may depend on configuration, authentication or interacting components. Examples motivate this hypothesis; unverified keyword percentages are omitted. Expertise and contextual dependence remain unmeasured. Attenuation after adjustment is consistent with composition contributing to raw topic separation.

Prior literature helps distinguish questions we should ask, not supply our conclusions. Allamanis and Sutton (2013) separate programming concepts from information sought. Beyer et al. (2020) compare automated approaches to seven question categories. Our overlapping LLM decisions are a local operationalization, not a replication of their supervised classifier or reported accuracy. We extend the discussion to response helpfulness and speed, with measurement uncertainty made explicit.

Descriptive patterns coexist with uncertain group differences and prompt-sensitive measurements. Speed, usable help and complete coverage remain distinct outcomes; more score variation alone does not validate a measurement.

8. Limitations and future work¶

The archive is historical and Python-specific. Answered-only selection, edits, uncertain code compatibility and non-random participation limit generalization. Classifications and ratings may share model biases; correlated errors can create or hide associations. Small groups and multiple comparisons limit precision. Neither regression adjustment nor illustrative cases identify causal mechanisms.

Next steps should formalize the existing manual checks with shared adjudication rules, and record per-ID recovery timestamps and model-weight versions. Future timing work should incorporate unanswered questions and observation windows rather than treat missing answers as ordinary delays. A conditional second-answer analysis should examine cases whose first answer was insufficient, not merely count the inevitable gain from observing more answers. These additions would strengthen interpretation more than simply maximizing sample size or searching for larger differences.
